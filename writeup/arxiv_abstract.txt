Spiking language models report large energy savings, usually by pricing a spike accumulation at 0.9 pJ against 4.6 pJ for a 32-bit floating-point multiply-accumulate. Several of them run integer activations as trains of unit spikes. We measure what that unrolling costs on a model where it is exact: RWKV-4-169M with ternary weights and 4-bit activations at every projection input, whose spike engine produces logits bit-identical to its integer engine. On the projections the usual formula credits spikes with 3.3x less energy; this factors into removed multiplications (5.1x) and skipped zeros (1.8x), both available to an integer engine with ternary weights, times the cost of the unrolling (0.36). Counted directly, unit spikes need 1.7-3.4 times the accumulations of a zero-skipping integer engine at every site, beat a dense engine only at the two sparse sites, and gain nothing from delta coding; the verdicts hold on TinyStories and WikiText-103. Under every event-driven cost model we could price, spikes cost more than the integer engine; where weights are held across a source's spikes, the answer reduces to the cost of a +/-1 increment, which published figures do not settle.
